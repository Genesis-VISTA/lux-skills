#### Key Take‑aways
1. **Robustness** – The selected design tolerates small asymmetries (δ ≲ 3 × 10⁻⁶) with only a ~1 % yield penalty.  
2. **Defect Sensitivity** – Larger asymmetries (δ = 0.3) or low‑mode RT perturbations (mode = 2) can reduce the yield by > 90 %.  
3. **Energy Scaling** – No laser‑energy sweep was performed, so the energy dependence of robustness remains unquantified.  
4. **Caveats** – The surrogate is a 0‑D deceleration model; it does not capture 3‑D jetting or laser‑plasma instability growth. The defect‑probe set is finite; a more aggressive adversary could still find a more damaging defect.

#### Next Steps
- **Energy Sweep** – Run a 1‑D sweep (e.g., 1–5 MJ) to map how the robust yield scales and to identify an optimal operating point.  
- **Defect Space Exploration** – Increase the number of defect probes, especially at higher δ and low modes, to ensure the design remains robust against the most damaging asymmetries.  
- **3‑D Validation** – Use a high‑fidelity 3‑D hydro code to confirm that the 0‑D robust design remains stable against jetting and other multi‑dimensional effects.
